Contributions are welcome.

This project is developed in the open, and thoughtful outside contributions are
part of what makes open source great. At the same time, maintainer attention
is limited, and reviewing a contribution can require substantially more time
than producing it.

For that reason, contributions should be made with care.

## Before opening a pull request

Small, focused fixes are generally welcome without prior discussion.

For larger changes, new features, architectural changes, broad refactors,
dependency changes, or anything that significantly increases the maintenance
surface of the project, please open an issue first.

Unsolicited large pull requests may be closed without detailed review.

A pull request should not be used as the first place to discover whether a
substantial change belongs in the project.

## Scope

Prefer the smallest change that solves the problem.

Avoid:

- unrelated cleanup
- broad refactors mixed with functional changes
- unnecessary abstractions
- speculative features
- large generated changes
- dependency additions without a clear reason

A change that is easy to produce but expensive to understand, review, or
maintain is not necessarily a useful contribution.

## Responsibility for your contribution

If you submit code, you are responsible for it.

You should be able to:

- explain what the change does
- explain why the change is necessary
- explain important implementation decisions
- identify relevant edge cases
- describe how the change was tested
- respond meaningfully to review feedback

This applies regardless of which tools were used to produce the contribution.

Use of AI-assisted development tools is not prohibited. However, submitting code
you do not understand is not acceptable.

The maintainer should not become the final debugging, verification, or reasoning
stage of an automated code-generation process.

## Review burden

Maintainer time is one of this project's scarcest resources.

Opening a pull request does not create an obligation for maintainers to perform
a detailed review.

A contribution may be closed if:

- its scope is too large
- it was not discussed beforehand when discussion was warranted
- it creates disproportionate review or maintenance burden
- its purpose is unclear
- the submitter cannot explain the implementation
- it does not align with the direction of the project

This is not necessarily a judgment on the contributor or on the technical
quality of the work. A change can be reasonable in isolation and still not be
appropriate for this project.

## What makes a good contribution

Good contributions usually have a few things in common:

- they solve a clear problem
- they are narrowly scoped
- they fit the existing design
- they include appropriate tests
- they avoid unnecessary complexity
- they are easy to understand
- they leave the project easier, not harder, to maintain

Bug reports with good reproductions, tests demonstrating existing problems,
documentation improvements, and careful investigation are also valuable
contributions.

Contribution is not measured in lines of code.

## Stewardship

This repository is open source, but project stewardship is curated.

Anyone is free to inspect, use, modify, and fork the code according to its
license. Upstream changes, however, are accepted at the discretion of the
maintainers.

The goal is not to maximize the number of contributions. The goal is to maintain
a coherent, reliable project over the long term.

Contributors who consistently demonstrate good judgment and familiarity with the
project will naturally be given more latitude over time.

## In short

Respect the project, respect the people who maintain it, and respect their time.

Submit changes you understand and are willing to stand behind.

Keep them focused.

For substantial work, talk to us before writing a large amount of code.

Thoughtful contributions are very welcome.
